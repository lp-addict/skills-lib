# Visual mapping

Choose a format that answers the question. A concept map can clarify distinctions, a sequence can compare recurring dreams, and a sketch invitation can explore a single image. Use the user's language for labels and a renderer the current surface supports. A written or tabular map also works.

For a theoretical psyche map, label it “Jung's conceptual model.” Place the ego in relation to consciousness, show the persona's relationship to social roles, and describe the Self as encompassing conscious and unconscious aspects. Label arrows with their meaning. Avoid making the Self a final stage, equating shadow with evil, or implying that a diagram represents anatomical locations.

For a personal map, distinguish the person's words from a proposed connection. Example: for three dreams about a station, lay out the actions in order (waiting, choosing a platform, boarding) and add the feeling the user reported beside each. Leave gaps blank. Only propose a change in attitude if their account supports it.

For a mandala or painting prompt, invite a choice of shape, colour or placement and ask about that choice if desired. Treat the result as a creative artifact. Do not score balance or symmetry as psychological health.
