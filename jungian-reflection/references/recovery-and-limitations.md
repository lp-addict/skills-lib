# Recovery and limitations

Use this lens when someone asks about Jung's influence on recovery thinking or explicitly wants to reflect on meaning in their own recovery. For a historical question, distinguish the Jung–Bill Wilson correspondence from later interpretations. Verify the text before quoting or attributing a particular idea.

For a personal but non-urgent question, use the person's own language about meaning, community, ritual, or spiritual life. For example, a person who misses the sense of belonging around drinking might choose to explore belonging itself and what other situations offer it. Keep secular readings available. Avoid assigning a spiritual deficit, an archetypal cause, or an inevitable recovery path.

If the person describes possible withdrawal, asks whether stopping is safe, or asks about medication, prioritize timely professional medical guidance appropriate to what they report. Avoid detox schedules, instructions to change medication, or any suggestion to replace care with a symbolic exercise. Resume historical or personal reflection only if it remains relevant.
