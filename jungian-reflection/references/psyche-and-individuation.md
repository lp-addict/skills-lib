# Psyche and individuation

Present these as terms in Jung's model. Define the distinction the user needs and illustrate it with one situation.

| Term | What the term does in the model |
|---|---|
| Ego | Names the standpoint from which one experiences oneself as a conscious “I.” |
| Self | Refers to the whole psyche and its organizing principle, including much outside conscious awareness. Reserve capital-S Self for this technical sense. |
| Persona | Describes the face a person develops for social roles. Its usefulness can become a constraint when one role feels like the whole person. |
| Shadow | Names qualities the conscious personality struggles to recognize as its own. An affectionate or ambitious side can belong here just as an aggressive side can. |
| Complex | Describes an emotionally charged grouping of associations that can organize a reaction before one has time to think. |
| Archetype | In Jung's theory, a disposition toward characteristic patterns of experience. Distinguish that proposed pattern from the specific image through which someone encounters it. |
| Individuation | Describes an ongoing attempt to live in fuller relation to aspects of oneself that pull in different directions. Avoid assigning a stage or rank. |

Example for persona and individuation. A dependable studio technician always fixes other people's work and seldom shows her own paintings. Her reliability can be authentic while her identification with being “the useful one” leaves little room for being seen as a beginner. Individuation might involve finding room for both roles. A shortage of time could also explain why she seldom paints.

For anima and animus, first explain Jung's historical formulation: he associated anima with an inner feminine figure in a man and animus with an inner masculine figure in a woman. For an interpretive alternative, explain that some later readers explore the encounter with inner otherness without assigning traits by sex or gender. Distinguish those positions. A dream figure's appearance cannot determine a person's identity, orientation or developmental stage.
